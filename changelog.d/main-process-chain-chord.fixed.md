- A `FAILURE` that Celery sends from a worker's main process (a hard time limit, a lost worker) now carries the task's chord (`chord_id` and `chord_callback_id`), as one from a task that raises already did. The steps after a failed chain step also fail for these failures, as they do for any other. Known limitation: when such a failure lands after the step's body returned (for example a time limit that hits in its `on_success` handler) and the step already sent the next one, every later step shows `FAILURE`, as in Celery's result backend. A later step that already finished keeps `FAILURE` over its `SUCCESS`. One still running or not yet sent shows `FAILURE` until its own result arrives, then `SUCCESS`. See [Graphs: failed chains](https://github.com/iansokolskyi/stemtrace/blob/main/docs/graphs.md).
